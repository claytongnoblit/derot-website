# DeRot — Positioning & Brand Brief

*Working draft for launch. Last updated June 30, 2026.*

---

## The one-line positioning

**DeRot is the calm-down app for the apps that wind you up.** It doesn't block your feeds or shame your screen time — it gives your nervous system a 60-second reset so you walk away from the scroll feeling settled instead of wired.

> **Category:** Digital wellbeing / nervous-system regulation (not "screen time control")
> **Tagline:** Regulate, don't restrict.

---

## Who it's for

**Primary:** The wellness-minded scroller. They already value calm — they breathe, stretch, maybe meditate, track their sleep. They don't think they have a "phone addiction," and they bristle at being treated like they do. What they notice is the *feeling*: they pick up the phone to check one thing, surface 20 minutes later jaw-clenched and vaguely agitated, and wish they felt better than that.

They are not looking to be locked out. They've tried (or dismissed) the hard blockers because being treated like a child who can't be trusted feels worse than the scrolling. They want a tool that respects their autonomy and works *with* their existing wellness habits rather than policing them.

**What they want:** to stay connected without paying the nervous-system tax. To feel like themselves after using their phone, not like they need to recover from it.

**Not the target (for launch):** the productivity/deep-work crowd chasing a focus score, and the "I've tried everything, lock me out completely" hard-block seeker. Those are Opal's and the blockers' users. Chasing them dilutes the calm positioning.

---

## The problem we name

Scrolling quietly spikes your nervous system. Your heart rate climbs, your shoulders creep up, your breath goes shallow — and you don't notice, because the content is holding your attention while your body does the stressing. You don't end a scroll session *informed*. You end it *activated*.

Every other app in this space treats the problem as **quantity** — too many opens, too many minutes — and fights it with friction or walls. DeRot treats the problem as **state**: the issue isn't only that you scrolled, it's how you *feel* afterward. We're the only one solving for the comedown.

---

## The core promise

You'll feel calmer on the other side of the scroll. Not blocked. Not lectured. Not staring at a guilt-trip usage chart. Just a quick, evidence-backed reset — slow exhale-weighted breathing that flips your vagus nerve and brings your heart rate down — built right into the apps that wound you up in the first place.

It's not woo. It's physiology. And it takes 60 seconds.

---

## Why we win (the wedge)

The whole category splits two ways, and DeRot sits in the gap between them:

**The hard blockers** (Opal, Screen Time) lock you out. Effective for some, but they feel punitive and infantilizing — and they make you the enemy of your own phone.

**The friction apps** (one sec, Clearspace, ScreenZen, Pauso) put a breath *before* the app opens, as a gate: "Do you still want to open this?" The breath is a speed bump — a means to make you reconsider and open less. one sec owns this lane outright, with a peer-reviewed study showing a 1-second delay cuts opens by ~57%.

**DeRot owns the part nobody else does: the breath as the actual point, not the gate.** Everyone else measures success in opens avoided. We measure it in how you feel when you put the phone down. We're not trying to win the argument at the door — we're sending you back to your life regulated. *Regulate, don't restrict.*

That reframe is also our moat: it's a different success metric, a different emotional contract with the user, and it's the one framing the wellness audience actually wants.

---

## Messaging pillars

Each pillar is a thing we can say repeatedly, prove, and build copy/features around.

**1. Regulate, don't restrict.**
We don't take your apps away. We change how they leave you feeling. This is the headline contract and the line that separates us from every blocker.

**2. It's physiology, not willpower.**
Slow, long-exhale breathing activates the vagus nerve and lowers heart rate — a real, measurable nervous-system shift. No discipline required; your body does the work. This makes the wellness crowd trust us and gives us a credible, non-preachy reason-to-believe.

**3. 60 seconds, then you're on your way.**
Fast, light, respectful of your time. We're a reset, not a detour. Lowers the cost of saying yes.

**4. Built into the apps you already use.**
The calm meets you where the stress happens — inside Instagram, TikTok, Reddit, X — not in a separate meditation app you have to remember to open.

**We are:** reassuring, plain-spoken, physiologically literate, a little wry.
**We are not:** clinical, preachy, alarmist, hustle-y, or guilt-trippy.

## Naming the benefit ladder

- **Feature:** exhale-weighted guided breathing (Calm 4-6, Box, 4-7-8, + custom on Pro), triggered inside chosen apps.
- **Functional benefit:** heart rate and arousal come down in ~60 seconds.
- **Emotional benefit:** you put the phone down feeling settled, not wired.
- **Identity benefit:** you're someone who stays connected *and* stays calm — no trade-off, no shame.

Lead with the emotional and identity rungs in consumer copy; keep the functional/physiology rung close behind as proof.

---

## Proof points / reasons to believe

- Long-exhale breathing → vagus nerve activation → lower heart rate (the Stanford 2023 cyclic-sighing / physiological-sigh research is the strongest single citation; exhale-emphasized breathing outperformed other techniques for calming).
- "Friction interrupts automaticity" is established (Max Planck / one sec ~57% study) — we can *acknowledge* the science of the pause while staking our claim on the comedown, not the gate.
- No account required, runs on-device — privacy as a quiet wellness signal.

## The mechanic (locked)

DeRot **interrupts you mid-scroll** — it catches you *after* the feed has already wound you up and walks you through a calm-down. There is deliberately **no "breathe before you open" gate.** We never stand between you and the app at the door.

This is a strategic choice, not just a technical one, and it's the cleanest possible fit for the positioning:
- We meet you at the moment of peak arousal — already activated, shoulders up, heart rate climbing — and bring you back down. That's the comedown we're built for.
- It keeps us out of one sec's lane entirely. They gate the open; we reset the scroll. Different moment, different promise, different category.

**Copy implication:** always describe the trigger as *"DeRot pauses your scroll to bring you back down,"* never *"breathe before you open."* The interruption is a calm hand on the shoulder mid-spiral, not a bouncer at the door.
