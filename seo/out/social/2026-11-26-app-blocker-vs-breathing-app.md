## Threads
Three ways a phone app can step in:

Block: cuts off access (Screen Time with a passcode, Opal, Brick).
Delay: adds a pause before opening (one sec, ScreenZen).
Regulate: helps you come down after the feed has wound you up.

One useful detail: in a controlled experiment, the 10-second delay added nothing beyond simply offering a way to back out.

## LinkedIn
App blockers, friction apps, and breathing apps get lumped together, but they do different jobs. A blocker cuts off access. A friction app adds a pause before opening. A breathing app aims to change how you feel once the feed has wound you up.

The research is more specific than most roundups admit. A 2023 PNAS field study of 280 one sec users (no control group) saw openings fall 57%, and a controlled experiment found the option to dismiss did most of the work. Breathing research points elsewhere: a meta-analysis of 12 trials found a small-to-moderate effect on stress, mostly from slow-paced breathing.

We wrote a neutral comparison of the three approaches, with prices as listed and if-then picks. We make DeRot, a breathing app, so it isn't ranked. If you want to be cut off completely, we say plainly that a blocker is the better fit. https://derot.org/blog/app-blocker-vs-breathing-app/
