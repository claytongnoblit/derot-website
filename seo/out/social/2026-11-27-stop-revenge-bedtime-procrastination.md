## Threads
Up at 1 am again, after scrolling to get some time back?

Wanting that time is reasonable. The trouble is the feed, which keeps your body on alert long after the fun has worn off.

Try this: when you notice you're still scrolling but no longer enjoying it, take three slow breaths (in for 4, out for 6). Then switch to a last thing you chose earlier, like a book or a few stretches.

## LinkedIn
Bedtime procrastination is a real research topic. A 2022 meta-analysis of 43 studies linked it to lower self-control, an evening chronotype, shorter sleep and daytime fatigue, though the authors rated the evidence as low quality. The "revenge" label is newer popular usage, but the feeling it names is familiar: the day took your time, so you take some back at night.

We don't think the fix is to give up the evening. The feed is what turns one hour into three. A cue-based check-in is more forgiving than a strict bedtime. When you notice you've stopped enjoying the scroll, take about 30 seconds of 4-6 breathing, then switch to a calm last thing you chose earlier.
