"Brain rot" is Oxford's word for the supposed decline in your mental state from too much trivial content. The research is early and mostly correlational, but the feeling is real.

A 2-minute way back down:
1. Jaw, shoulders, breath. Unclench and drop.
2. Inhale 4, exhale 6, for 6 rounds.
3. Look at something far away for 40 seconds.

## LinkedIn
Brain rot isn't a diagnosis. It's internet slang for the foggy, overstimulated feeling after a long run of short-form video, and a 2025 meta-analysis of 71 studies did link heavier use with weaker attention. Most of those studies were correlational, though, so the long-term story is still open.

The more useful finding: time spent was only weakly linked with cognition, while compulsive use and emotional dependency on the feed were linked much more strongly. How you scroll seems to matter more than how long.

So our guide to de-rotting your brain starts with your state, not your screen time: notice the tension mid-scroll, take 60 seconds of slow 4-6 breathing, rest your eyes on something distant, then add real-world time.
