That's why we prefer a reset to a rule. A ban asks you to never get wound up. A short pause meets you once you already are, which is when you actually need help.

An honest note: most research linking doomscrolling to lower well-being is correlational. It shows a connection, not a direction. You don't need a causal study to notice you feel worse after twenty minutes of bad news, though.

Breathing and pauses can help you feel calmer in the moment, but they don't replace real support. If checking the news keeps feeding your worry or getting in the way of sleep, talk to a doctor or mental health professional.

Most advice about doomscrolling targets minutes. But the real problem is a state: the point where the feed has wound you up and you keep going anyway.

So meet that moment instead of trying to ban it. When you notice a tight jaw, raised shoulders or shallow breath, take your thumb off the screen and look up. Breathe in through your nose for 4 and out slowly for 6, three times. That's about 30 seconds. Then ask whether you want more of this right now, and choose on purpose.

Keep it gentle, and stop if you feel dizzy.
