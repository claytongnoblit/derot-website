"Take a deep breath" can backfire. The calming part of a breath is the pace, not the size.

Rapid or forceful breathing is activating. It has its place, like getting energy before exercise. But doing breath of fire after 20 minutes of comment sections is a bit like fixing a loud room by turning up the music.

What leans you toward rest and digest, roughly in order of evidence: a slow rate (around six breaths a minute), an exhale longer than the inhale, breathing through your nose, and a soft belly. Rate has the strongest support. Nose breathing has small, early support. A soft belly hasn't been tested on its own, but it gives the breath somewhere low and quiet to go.

In a 2025 study of 84 students, breathing at six breaths a minute raised heart rate variability more than box breathing or 4-7-8.

The best time to use it is the moment you notice the feed has wound you up: jaw tight, thumb still moving. Three slow breaths is enough to start coming back down. "Take a deep breath" sounds calming, but big, forceful breaths can leave you more wired. What helps is slow and gentle: about six breaths a minute, with an exhale longer than the inhale.

Try this the next time a scroll leaves your jaw tight. Drop your shoulders, soften your belly, breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds and out for 6. Three breaths takes about 30 seconds.

Keep each breath normal-sized. If you feel dizzy or tingly, stop and make them smaller. If anxiety or distress persist, talk to a doctor or mental health professional.
