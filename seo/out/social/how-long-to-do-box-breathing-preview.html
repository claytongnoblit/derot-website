You picked up your phone to check one thing. Twenty minutes later, your shoulders are up near your ears.

That's the feed doing what feeds do. Four rounds of box breathing takes about a minute, and for taking the edge off, a minute may well be enough.

Want a longer session? In the best-known study, a 2023 Stanford trial, 108 people practiced five minutes a day for 28 days. Across the breathing groups, mood gains grew with the number of days people practiced. Box breathing didn't significantly beat mindfulness, and heart rate variability didn't change. A modest, gentle effect, not an off switch.

A realistic habit: Stanford's breathwork participants averaged under six minutes a day and practiced on about 20 of the 28 days. They missed days and their mood gains still grew.

Two practical notes. Reaction times may be slower right after a session, so give yourself a short pause before driving. At bedtime, keep the counts short and gentle, since some people find the holds a little alerting at night.

Box breathing can help you feel calmer. If stress or anxiety is persistent, or sleep keeps being hard, talk to a professional.

How long should you do box breathing for? For a quick reset, four rounds of 4-4-4-4 is enough, and that takes about one minute. For a fuller session, aim for about five minutes. Once or twice a day is a common recommendation.

You don't have to finish a set number of rounds. Once your breath slows on its own and your shoulders drop, you can stop. If the 4-second holds feel strained, shorten them to 3 or 2, or skip the hold after the exhale. Stop if you feel dizzy or lightheaded.
