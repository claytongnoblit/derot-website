The feed has wound you up. Coming back down can take less than a minute.

Here's a 30-second script for mid-scroll: put the phone face-down (no need to put it away), do two or three slow sighs, check your jaw and shoulders, then breathe easily for about 30 seconds. After that, decide whether to pick the phone back up. The feed will be exactly where you left it.

Your body already does this on its own. People sigh spontaneously about every five minutes, and in one lab study those natural sighs were followed by lower muscle tension. A deliberate sigh tries to borrow that reflex, which is why we keep it gentle and few.

If breathing out through your mouth feels odd, nose-only sighing works too. The Stanford protocol allowed it.

Want the daily practice instead? About five minutes of cyclic sighing is the dose that was studied. Our free physiological sigh timer paces it so you don't have to count.

A safety note: do it sitting or standing still, never while driving. If you're pregnant, have a heart or lung condition, a history of fainting, or find breath focus triggers panic, go gently or check with a clinician first. It isn't a treatment, and for persistent anxiety or sleep trouble, talk to a professional.

How long should you do a physiological sigh? For a quick reset, one to three sighs, about 10 to 30 seconds. For a daily practice, about five minutes of cyclic sighing, the only dose tested in a trial.

The breath: inhale through your nose for 2 to 3 seconds, take a small top-up sip, then exhale slowly through your mouth for 6 to 8 seconds. You've done enough when your exhale lengthens by itself and your jaw and shoulders drop. Stop right away if you feel tingly or lightheaded.
