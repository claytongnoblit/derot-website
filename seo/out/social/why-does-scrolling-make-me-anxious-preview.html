You picked up your phone to check one thing. Twenty minutes later your teeth are pressed together.

Nothing bad actually happened to you. But the feed stacks threat-heavy headlines, other people's highlights, constant switching and stories that never end. Your body can react to that steady stream of small alarms as if they mattered.

How you sit plays a part too. Curled over the phone, the breath tends to sit high and short, and a body breathing like that can feel more on edge.

We like resetting mid-scroll because that's when the jaw is tight and the breath is high. A rule you set before opening an app doesn't meet you there. You don't have to close anything or make big decisions. Just pause.

Done gently, you'll usually feel your shoulders slide down and your exhale lengthen on its own. You're not aiming for bliss, just one notch down from wired. If 6 counts feels like a strain, try in for 3, out for 5.

DeRot, coming soon to iOS, pauses your apps while you reset with a few calming breaths.

Breathing can help you feel calmer in the moment. It isn't a substitute for care. If the anxious feeling sticks around after you put the phone down, it's worth talking to a professional.

One finding we keep coming back to: in 2021 experiments, a few minutes of COVID news lowered people's positive mood, but stories about kindness didn't. Same platforms, similar time.

So it's not only the minutes. When you notice your jaw is tight mid-scroll, try five breaths: in through your nose for 4, out slowly for 6. About 50 seconds.

You pick up your phone to check one message, and twenty minutes later your jaw is tight, your shoulders are up and there's a low hum of unease you can't place. That's a common experience, and it's usually the feed, not you.

The research is honest but modest: in 2021 experiments, a few minutes of COVID-19 news lowered positive mood while kindness stories did not. What you see seems to matter at least as much as how long you look.

A simple reset for the moment you notice it: put the phone down, let your shoulders drop, and take five slow breaths, in for 4 and out for 6. That's about 50 seconds. Stop and breathe normally if you feel dizzy.
