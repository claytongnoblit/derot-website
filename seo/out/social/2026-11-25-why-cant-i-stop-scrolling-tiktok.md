## Threads
Why is TikTok so hard to put down? It opens straight into full-screen autoplay, has no bottom, and keeps serving videos picked for you. You never choose to continue, only to stop.

When you notice you're 20 minutes in, try this: phone face down, unclench your jaw, inhale through your nose for 4, exhale slowly for 6. Three rounds, about 30 seconds. Then close the app or keep watching on purpose.

We're building DeRot around that mid-scroll moment.

## LinkedIn
TikTok isn't hard to put down because people lack willpower. A 2025 Baylor study found people rated it more effortless and better at recommendations than Reels or Shorts, and the feed has no natural stopping point.

What's rarely mentioned: most of the research linking short video to stress and anxiety is correlational, and in-app reminders to stop fade fast. In one field study, people kept scrolling an average of 3.5 minutes after a 15-minute prompt.

Our new explainer covers the design, what the evidence does and doesn't show, and a 30-second breathing reset (inhale 4, exhale 6) for the moment you notice. It's the moment DeRot is built for: it pauses your apps while you reset with a few calming breaths.

[2-7s] VO: It's not willpower. TikTok opens into autoplay and never ends. Stopping is the only choice you have to make.
[7-12s] VO: Notice your jaw? Your shoulders? That wired feeling is common after a long scroll.
[12-17s] VO: Pop-ups telling you to stop fade fast. So change how your body feels first.
[17-28s] Text + VO: Phone face down. Unclench. Breathe in through your nose for 4. Out slowly for 6. Three rounds.
[28-30s] Text: Then choose: close it, or keep watching on purpose.
